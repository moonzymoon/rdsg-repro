\begin{abstract}{\sloppy
Per-channel monitors miss failures reshaping inter-channel dependence,
leaving marginals intact. We introduce the rough dependency structure
graph (RDSG), monitoring the dependency degree $\gamma$ and direction
$\delta$ on copula-standardised inter-channel edges at a calibrated
false-alarm budget.
On a synthetic gate the family reaches 0.47 recall against the
$\gamma$-only ablation's 0.36. Point baselines sit at their floors. On WADI, RDSG alone
registers recall at the budgeted operating point (5/14 attacks). At a
common requested budget it exceeds the strongest external baseline on two of three
networks (by exact paired differences $0.27$ and $0.09$), the Gaussian leader
ahead on the third. Short events
remain hardest.
}\end{abstract}

\keywords{rough sets; dependency degree; multivariate time series;
relational anomaly detection; false-alarm calibration}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Monitoring systems rarely fail by moving one signal at a time. A pump and its
upstream pressure sensor keep their marginal distributions while their
coupling inverts; two traffic sensors that never correlated before start
moving together; a controller loop quietly severs the statistical tie between
command and response. In all three cases every per-channel alarm, and every
learned detector whose score is a fixed function of the single-channel value,
keeps silent, because the evidence lives in the \emph{joint} distribution, not
in the marginals. We call these failures \emph{relational anomalies} and the
task of detecting them \emph{relational anomaly detection}.

The rough-set notion of dependency \citep{pawlak1982,ziarko1993} is a natural
lens for this task: it asks, for a set of condition attributes and a decision
attribute, how much of the decision is determined by the conditions. Placed on
a multivariate time series, the question ``does the granular past of channel
$i$ determine the granular present of channel $j$?'' is exactly a dependency
question, and its negation over time, where a dependency appears, disappears
or reverses, is a relational anomaly. Yet the classical dependency degree
$\gamma$ carries a known blind spot that is fatal here: when $Y=X$ turns into
$Y=-X$, every granule of $X$ still determines the same granule of $Y$, the
positive region does not shrink, and $\gamma$ does not move. A detector built
on $\gamma$ alone is deaf to inversion, one of the most damaging relational
failures.

This paper builds the detector that the blind spot forbids, in three moves.

\textbf{(i) A four-tuple edge encoding.} Each candidate dependency edge
$(A\!\to\! d,\tau)$, where $A$ is a channel subset, $d$ a target, and $\tau$ a lag,
is encoded by the dependency degree, a rule-mapping consistency $\rho$, a granular
direction coefficient $\delta$ (statistical direction, not causal) and a
conditional-distribution divergence
$D_{JS}$. We prove by construction that no single pair-level component covers all
three canonical relation changes, each of which some other component or
edge level sees (\S\ref{sec:quad}). $\gamma$ is
taken in a soft variable-precision form, namely the occupancy-weighted modal rule
confidence, which is defined for real-valued series and, crucially, is
\emph{stable under inversion}, so that the remaining components carry exactly
the information $\gamma$ must miss.

\textbf{(ii) Copula standardisation.} All statistics are computed on
contingency tables whose margins are first equalised by a smoothed
iterative-proportional-fitting (SIPF) transform. Without this step the dependency
degree inherits a target channel's marginal occupancy skew and reports
dependencies that do not exist (measured artefact $0.39$, floor $0.25$). With it, the independence floor of the fully standardised table is
$1/K$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:bounds}); the two-iteration implementation
approximates exact uniformity, and residual marginal leakage is
quantified in S.8.

\textbf{(iii) A dual-channel candidate graph with honest calibration.} A
detector that scans every pair $\times$ lag $\times$ statistic pays a family
tax that buries real signals. The rough dependency structure graph
(\RDSG{}) instead maintains a compact
\emph{rupture graph} of reference-significant edges (for breakdown and
inversion) and a small \emph{emergence pool} of weak pairs (for newly born
dependencies), defines the graph once at the primary granularity, and
calibrates per-component alarm thresholds by block permutation
\citep{kunsch1989} and an empirical self-scan of the reference segment, so
that the reference-segment false-alarm rate is an input to the procedure. An optional
rolling-mean variant (library option, unexercised, S.8) exploits the
persistence of a true rupture across consecutive boundaries.

Each alarm additionally reports which component fired and on which channel,
giving the operator a component-level attribution (an exploratory type label
is provided in the repository but is not claimed as a capability; \S\ref{sec:e2}).

The contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item a granular decision-table construction that turns channel-subset$\times$lag
dependence into rough-set quantities computable by counting, with
copula-standardised statistics with an exact independence floor on the
ideal standardised table;
\item a four-component edge encoding that describes the relation change
from complementary perspectives, of which two components form the
calibrated detection family;
\item a dual-channel candidate-graph monitor whose alarm thresholds are
calibrated by reference self-scan under a stated false-alarm budget, with
block-permuted nulls for edge attribution; and
\item a margin-preserving injection suite and FA-matched protocol under
which point, graph-deviation and structural monitors are evaluated on three
real networks and two labelled ICS logs.
\end{enumerate}

Proposition~\ref{prop:blind} in §\ref{sec:quad}
formalises the complementary blind spots. Propositions~\ref{prop:bounds}--\ref{prop:granular} and Lemma~\ref{lemma:margin} establish the bounds,
crisp-table correspondence, inversion invariance, coarsening monotonicity,
and the margin-preservation guarantee (proofs of
Propositions~\ref{prop:bounds}--\ref{prop:granular} and of the lemma, and
the full constructions behind Proposition~\ref{prop:blind}, are
given in the Supplemental Material).

We evaluate the method on a margin-preserving injection suite (re-orderings of
observed values inside a window: marginals are preserved point-wise, so any
marginal-based detector is provably blind), on three real sensor networks
(METR-LA, Beijing multi-site air quality, PEMS-BAY) and on the MSU gas
pipeline with 53 labelled attacks. The headline findings are:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item the detection family ($\gamma$ and $\delta$) outperforms its $\gamma$-only ablation at the calibrated operating point
      (\eZeroRdsRecall{} vs.\ \eZeroGammaRecall{} overall; inversion
      \eZeroRdsInv{} vs.\ \eZeroGammaInv{}), confirming that the blind-spot
      fix is load-bearing, not decorative;
\item with false alarms matched on the reference segment, point detectors
      (Isolation Forest \citep{liu2008isolation} and LOF \citep{breunig2000lof} aggregations) and a graph deviation network
      collapse towards their floors on all relational injection types
      (IF \eZeroIfRecall{}, LOF $0.04$, MI-graph $0.06$), while \RDSG{}
      retains a recall of \eZeroRdsRecall{} ($p=\eZeroPval$, Wilcoxon);
\item on the gas pipeline, \RDSG{} traces an operating curve that dominates
      linear-Gaussian baselines at low false-alarm budgets, the regime that
      matters operationally. On the three real networks RDSG significantly exceeds the strongest external baseline on two of three
networks (one-sided Wilcoxon, $p\le 0.007$), METR-LA over every
external baseline and PEMS-BAY over the LOF aggregation; on air quality
the strongest baseline is nominally ahead and the $\gamma$-only
ablation leads both; on WADI real cyber-attacks it is, among methods
evaluable at that budget, the only one with nonzero recall.
\item an initial capability gate exposes precisely which statistical choices
      matter (soft $\gamma$, copula standardisation, family design, component
      thresholds); we document the diagnostic chain because each step
      generalises to other granular detectors.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Scope and non-goals}
\RDSG{} reports data-internal relational anomalies with type attribution. It
does not trigger controller retraining (complementary work on detector-health
monitoring owns that decision), does not calibrate delayed labels (owned by
delay-calibration work), and uses its permutation machinery for detection, not
for benchmark auditing. These interfaces are stated so the method composes
with, rather than duplicates, those functions.

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Rough sets and outlier/anomaly detection.} Rough-set outlier
detection reaches back to \citet{pawlak1982}'s approximations and their first
generalisations \citep{dubois1990}, and has grown
along several lines: entropy- and neighbourhood-based detectors in
multidimensional data \citep{hu2008neighborhood,tsmc2022entropy}, granular-ball constructions for
mixed and high-dimensional data \citep{tkde2026granularball}, multi-view and
multi-granularity fusions \citep{ijar2025multiview}, and incremental granular
self-organising models for novelty detection \citep{ins2027granularsom}.
Semi-supervised and weakly supervised rough detectors
\citep{asoc2024consistency} address label scarcity. Closest to our evaluation
question, a recent family of rough-set anomaly detectors scores individual
samples. Representative members include adaptive-neighbourhood kNN rough sets
\citep{chen2025adknrs}, fuzzy-rough mean-shift clustering
\citep{liao2025fusing}, and a rough-set-inspired isolation forest
\citep{rachwal2025rsif}. A neighbourhood-coverage representative of this
family serves as a baseline in \S\ref{sec:e2}. Within the systems and
cybernetics literature, rough-set data analysis was classically established
by \citet{pawlak1998cs}, and three-way decision models more recently extend
granular rough sets to multi-attribute decisions
\citep{qiao2024threeway}; these treat decisions over samples, not the
evolution of dependence between channels. All of these score
\emph{samples} against a sample distribution; none models dependence
\emph{between channels} as the monitored object. The closest rough-set works
on time series use rough models for early-warning classification
\citep{scirep2026dynamic} or credibility-adjusted clustering
\citep{eswa2027credibility}; both remain at the per-series level, and neither
targets structural change of inter-channel dependence. \RDSG{} differs in its
monitored object (edges of a dependency graph), its statistics (a
copula-standardised four-tuple around the dependency degree), and its output
(typed alarms with per-edge attribution).

\textbf{Dependency-aware detection in multivariate series.} Learning-based
approaches infer channel graphs and score deviations on them: the graph
deviation network of \citet{deng2021gdn}, graph-attention detectors
\citep{zhao2020mtadgat}, and causal-graph detectors built on (conditional)
independence and transfer-entropy ideas \citep{runge2019pcmci}. These learn a
graph once
(or slowly) and treat deviations of values from predictions. The graph itself
is not the monitored hypothesis, and no statistical guarantee on relational
alarms is offered. Bayesian change-point models of changing dependency
structure \citep{xuan2007changing} are the closest classical statement of our
problem. They assume conjugate parametric families and degrade with
non-stationary marginals. \citet{granger1969}'s causality testing is the
classical pairwise tool. We use it as a baseline and share its candidate-pair
discipline, but replace parametric F-tests by granular statistics that need no
linearity.

\textbf{Learning-based monitors.} Deep learning approaches form a large
family: stochastic recurrent models \citep{su2019omni}, association-discrepancy
transformers \citep{xu2022anomaly}, forecasters \citep{tuli2022tranad}, and
multi-instance attention scoring \citep{jang2024cs} --- the last closest
to our setting: learned attention over prediction residuals, versus
counting on granular tables with no training. The graph-based sub-field (\emph{G-TSAD}) is
systematically surveyed by \citet{ho2025gtsad}, who classify anomalies
into five categories (node, edge, sub-graph, graph, inter-observation)
and note that \emph{edge and sub-graph anomalies in
constructed graphs}, precisely the relational failures this paper
targets, remain the least explored category. Methods such as GDN
\citep{deng2021gdn} and correlation-aware spatial--temporal graph learning
\citep{zheng2024cstgl} learn a graph as a \emph{representation} for value
prediction. In contrast,
\RDSG{} treats the dependency graph as the \emph{monitored object}: edges
are rough-set dependency degrees computed on granular decision tables,
alarms are calibrated statistical tests on graph structure, and alarms carry
type attribution. This distinction, graph-as-representation vs.
graph-as-hypothesis, is fundamental and, to our knowledge, has no counterpart in the
G-TSAD literature. Graph structure also serves a third role, downstream of
detection: grounding anomaly \emph{explanation}, with causal graphs
enforcing the structural compliance of LLM-generated root-cause narratives
\citep{zhang2026exsy70467}, constraining interpretation rather than
raising alarms. Change-point methodology attacks dependency structure
from the statistical side, both parametrically
\citep{xuan2007changing,killick2012pelt} and nonparametrically for
multivariate energy-based partitions \citep{matteson2014ecp}, with offline
methods systematically surveyed by \citet{truong2020cpss};
typically one series or a parametrically modelled series at a time.
The rough-set outlier detection literature
\citep{tsmc2022entropy,tkde2026granularball,ijar2025multiview,asoc2024consistency}
scores individual samples rather than inter-channel dependence. \RDSG{} occupies exactly that gap: its monitored objects
are the survey's \emph{edge} and \emph{sub-graph} anomaly classes, detected
statistically rather than learned.

\textbf{Statistical machinery.} Our calibration composes block permutations
\citep{kunsch1989} with an empirical self-scan; multiplicity is controlled by
per-component families with union-bound budgets rather than a global
Benjamini--Hochberg sweep \citep{benjamini1995}, which we reserve for
post-alarm edge attribution. Mutual information
\citep{kraskov2004}, partial correlation and VAR residual monitors
\citep{granger1969} are treated as baselines under the same false-alarm
matching protocol. Our event-level, false-alarm-matched evaluation also
follows the direction of recent multivariate TSAD benchmarking efforts,
which have moved away from point-wise scores towards event-level and
budget-aware protocols.

\subsection*{A cybernetic reading}
In systems terms, the monitor proposed here occupies the \emph{diagnose}
stage of the classical loop \emph{observe $\rightarrow$ diagnose $\rightarrow$
act}: observation is delegated to existing per-channel and learned monitors,
acting (retraining, failover, maintenance) is delegated to the operator or to
downstream decision services. The contribution is the middle layer, a
statistical instrument that turns granular dependence into a falsifiable
alarm with a type label, together with the calibration discipline that
makes its alarm rate an input rather than an outcome. The type label
matters for the act stage: a \emph{breakdown} suggests a severed
communication or failed coupling, an \emph{inversion} suggests a
sign-reversed feedback loop (the classic stability hazard), and an
\emph{emergence} suggests an unauthorised coupling or side-channel.
Framed this way, the detector composes with, rather than competes
against, detector-health and retraining services described in the
industrial monitoring literature. In control-theoretic terms, the
dependency graph is a structural model of the observed system; a
rupture alarm signals that the model's structure, not its parameters,
has changed, which is the regime where gain scheduling and parameter
adaptation are insufficient and re-identification or failover is
required. The component-level attribution (which statistic fired, on
which channel) maps directly to the observability-redundancy analysis
used in fault diagnosis: a direction flip suggests a sign error or
sensor reversal, while a degree collapse suggests a communication or
physical coupling failure.

\section{Preliminaries and problem statement}\label{sec:prelim}

Let $x(t)\in\mathbb{R}^n$, $t=1,\dots,T$, be an $n$-channel series. A
\emph{reference segment} $W_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is assumed to hold one stationary
relational regime. A \emph{test segment} is monitored. A \emph{relational
anomaly} is an interval in the test segment during which there exist a channel
subset $A$, a target channel $d$ and a lag $\tau$ such that the dependence of
the granulated $d(t)$ on the granulated $A(t-\tau)$ differs systematically
from the reference regime, while each channel's marginal distribution is
approximately unchanged. Three types are distinguished: \emph{breakdown} (a
reference dependence vanishes), \emph{inversion} (a reference dependence
persists in strength but flips its rule or direction), and \emph{emergence} (a
dependence appears; if it exists only jointly in a subset and not in any pair,
we call it subset-joint emergence).

A \emph{granular decision table} for target $d$ and lag $\tau$ over a window
$W$ has one object per time $t\in W$, condition attributes
$a_i(t)=\Phi^K_i(x_i(t-\tau))\in\{0,\dots,K-1\}$ for $i\in A$ (equal-frequency
granulation $\Phi^K_i$ fitted on the reference segment and frozen) and
decision attribute $\delta_d(t)=\Phi^K_d(x_d(t))$. The induced contingency
table $T\in\mathbb{N}^{K^{|A|}\times K}$ counts granule pairs. \emph{All
statistics below are functions of $T$ only}, which is what makes block
permutation cheap.

\section{The rough dependency structure graph (RDSG)}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Overview}\label{sec:overview}
Given a multivariate series $x(t) \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and a reference
segment $W_{\mathrm{ref}}$, the pipeline proceeds in five steps.
First, each channel is granulated into $K$ equal-frequency levels to
produce grain sequences $G = \Phi^K(x)$.
Second, candidate dependency edges $(A, d, \tau)$ are selected from the
reference segment into a dual-channel graph (Table~\ref{tab:symbols}).
Third, each edge's four-tuple $(\gamma, \rho, \delta, D_{JS})$ is
computed on copula-standardised contingency tables.
Fourth, alarm thresholds are calibrated on the reference segment so the
false-alarm rate is an input.
Fifth, alarms are merged into events and typed.
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} illustrates the pipeline: panel (a) shows the dual-channel candidate graph, panel (b) the copula-standardised statistics that feed the detection family, and panel (c) the rate-calibrated alarm machinery; the complete procedure is tabulated as Table~\ref{alg:rdsg}.

\subsection{Copula-standardised granular statistics}\label{sec:quad}

\paragraph{Standardisation.} Raw table margins carry the channel's occupancy
skew: we measured a channel whose dependency degree against \emph{every}
partner sat at $0.39$, a pure marginal artefact against a floor of $0.25$.
We therefore equalise margins with one pass of additive smoothing
($\alpha{=}8$ counts, so sparse rows shrink towards uniform instead of
amplifying sampling noise) followed by two iterations of
iterative proportional fitting to the uniform margins (the SIPF
transform). The result $\pi(T)$ is
a discrete copula estimate: every statistic below is a function of the
standardised table(s) ($\pi_{\mathrm{c}}$ alone, or the reference--current
pair $\pi_{\mathrm{r}},\pi_{\mathrm{c}}$ for $\rho$ and $D_{JS}$), so
that exact fitting of a margin-balanced table attains the $1/K$ floor and
marginal drift does not move the statistics; on skewed independent tables
the smoothing pass itself shifts the floor slightly --- a shift stable in
the iteration count, absorbed by the calibration's empirical null (S.8).

\paragraph{Four components.} For reference and current tables
$T_{\mathrm{r}},T_{\mathrm{c}}$ with standardised versions
$\pi_{\mathrm{r}},\pi_{\mathrm{c}}$:
\begin{align}
\gamma(\pi) &= \sum_{g}\pi_g\,\max_{v}\pi_{v\mid g}
  &&\text{(soft variable-precision dependency degree)}\\
\rho(T_{\mathrm{r}},T_{\mathrm{c}}) &=
  \sum_{g}\pi^{\mathrm{r}}_g\,\mathbf{1}\!\left[\phi_{\mathrm{r}}(g)=\phi_{\mathrm{c}}(g)\right]
  &&\text{(modal rule-map consistency)}\\
\delta(\pi) &= \mathrm{corr}\big(g,\;v;\;\pi\big)
  &&\text{(granular direction, centred indices)}\\
D_{JS}(\pi_{\mathrm{r}},\pi_{\mathrm{c}}) &=
  \sum_{g}\pi^{\mathrm{r}}_g\,\mathrm{JSD}\big(\pi^{\mathrm{r}}_{\cdot\mid g},
  \pi^{\mathrm{c}}_{\cdot\mid g}\big)
  &&\text{(conditional residual divergence)}
\end{align}
with $\phi(g)=\arg\max_v\pi_{v\mid g}$ the modal decision rule.
$\gamma\in[1/K,1]$ is the occupancy-weighted confidence of the modal rules.
It is the soft variable-precision reading of the dependency degree
\citep{ziarko1993} and is what remains computable on real-valued series where
hard $\beta$-positive regions are empty. The tuple
$s(A\!\to\! d,\tau)=(\gamma,\rho,\delta,D_{JS})$ is the edge state.

\begin{proposition}[Blind spots are complementary]\label{prop:blind}
(i) If $Y=X$ changes to $Y=-X$ with symmetric granulation, then
$\gamma$ is unchanged and, for even $K$, $\rho$
drops to its floor while $|\Delta\delta|$ (with odd $K$ the
central granule keeps its rule)
is maximal. (ii) If the conditional law of $Y$ given $X=g$ is perturbed for
a single $g$ occupying mass $\pi_g$, by $\epsilon$ in total variation, then
$\gamma$ moves by at most $O(\pi_g\epsilon)$ while $D_{JS}$, on
$\alpha$-smoothed tables with positive cells, moves by
$\Theta(\pi_g\epsilon^2)$. (iii) For a subset-joint dependence
$x_d=f(x_{i_1},x_{i_2})$ with $f$ the parity of two independent, uniformly
distributed parent granules, no pair-edge statistic moves
off its null value ($\gamma$, $\delta$ and $D_{JS}$ stay at
their independence floors and $\rho$ compares two noise modal maps). Hence no single pair-level component covers the family
{breakdown, inversion, subset-joint emergence}: by (i) $\gamma$ is
blind to inversion while $\rho$ drops to its floor and $|\Delta\delta|$ is maximal; by (ii) $\gamma$ moves by at most $O(\pi_g\epsilon)$ on reshaping while $D_{JS}$ responds at order $\Theta(\pi_g\epsilon^2)$ (neither $\rho$ nor $\delta$ is bounded by (ii)); and by (iii) every
pair-level statistic is blind to subset-joint emergence, which the subset
edges recover (Figure~\ref{fig:coverage}).
\end{proposition}

\noindent\emph{Proof sketch.} (i) With symmetric reference quantiles,
granulation commutes with negation ($g(-x)=K-1-g(x)$): the injected table
is the reference table with columns reversed, so $\pi$, the row
confidences $c_g$, and $\gamma$ are unchanged, while the modal map becomes
$v\mapsto K-1-v$, dropping $\rho$ on the moved rows and flipping the sign
of $\delta$. (ii) Redistributing off-modal mass inside a granule
$g^\ast$ pins the modal confidence, so $\gamma$ moves by at most
$\pi_{g^\ast} O(\epsilon)$ while the conditional law of $g^\ast$ moves by
$\Theta(\epsilon)$ in total variation, hence $D_{JS}$, whose cells are
positive after $\alpha$-smoothing, by
$\Theta(\pi_{g^\ast}\epsilon^2)$. (iii) For a parity map
$x_d=f(x_{i_1},x_{i_2})$ of two independent, uniformly distributed parent
granules, every pair table has
independent margins and uniform conditionals, so all pair statistics sit
at their null values while the subset edge is deterministic ($\gamma=1$). Constructions (i)-(iii) give: $\gamma$ blind to inversion (i) and
bounded at order $\pi_g\epsilon$ under reshaping (ii), with neither $\rho$ nor $\delta$ constrained by (ii); and every pair-level
statistic blind to the subset-joint construction (iii), which the subset
edge recovers. No single pair-level component therefore covers the three
canonical changes.
Full constructions are given in the Supplemental Material (S.6).

remains pair-level; the $\gamma$ column alone misses
inversion.}\label{fig:coverage}\end{figure}

Empirically, Proposition~\ref{prop:blind} predicts that a
$\gamma$-only detector will miss inversion events while the full
encoding detects them, a prediction confirmed in §\ref{sec:gate}
(Table~\ref{tab:e0}). Four further properties of the standardised
degree (the bounds $1/K\le\gamma\le1$, the crisp-table correspondence
with the Pawlak degree, inversion invariance, and coarsening
monotonicity) are stated in §\ref{sec:grounding}
(Propositions~\ref{prop:bounds}--\ref{prop:granular}, proofs in the
Supplemental Material), and a margin-preservation lemma in
§\ref{sec:protocol} states that any per-channel score is invariant under
our injections in the window-level sense, so that a point detector's
detection rate on injected windows equals its window-level false-alarm
rate (Lemma~\ref{lemma:margin}).

We state them together because the \emph{design
consequence} is the point: \emph{detection} uses the two continuous
difference components $\Delta\gamma$ and $\Delta\delta$ only. Two further
exclusions are deliberate. $\rho$ is excluded because its discrete jumps
inflate family maxima. $D_{JS}$ is excluded for a structural reason. On
the reference-significant edges that \RDSG{} monitors, the relation is
close to deterministic. Adjacent sliding windows then induce almost
identical conditional tables, $D_{JS}$ degenerates to zero under the null,
and its sliding-split standard deviation vanishes, so no $z$-standardisation
of the kind the family thresholding requires is well defined. $D_{JS}$ is
therefore an \emph{attribution and typing} statistic. It responds
strongly once a relation is reshaped, which is exactly the regime
Proposition~\ref{prop:blind}(ii) describes, but it cannot serve as a
calibrated detection component. Detection power against the inversion
blind spot is carried by $\delta$: dropping it re-instates the blind spot
measurably (\S\ref{sec:ablation}). The converse does not
hold. The direction component is necessary for inversion but not
exclusive to it, because on a directional edge a breakdown also returns
$\delta$ toward zero. The component-firing matrix of \S\ref{sec:e2}
exhibits exactly this asymmetry. The four statistics remain jointly
informative. Proposition~\ref{prop:blind} is a statement about the edge
state, not about which of its components enter the calibrated family. The
division of roles (two components detect, two interpret) is itself a design
consequence of that proposition.
\subsection{Relation to classical dependency degrees}\label{sec:grounding}
The soft degree is not an analogue of the rough-set dependency degree but
its confidence-weighted extension. We first collect the four formal
properties of the standardised degree used throughout; full proofs are
given in the Supplemental Material (S.1--S.4).

\begin{proposition}[Bounds and extrema]\label{prop:bounds}
Let $\pi$ be a copula-standardised granular table over $K$ decision granules.
Then $1/K \le \gamma(\pi) \le 1$. Also $\gamma(\pi)=1$ iff every occupied
row is deterministic (all mass concentrated in one decision granule), and
$\gamma(\pi)=1/K$ iff every occupied row is uniform over the $K$ decision
granules.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Crisp-table correspondence with the Pawlak degree]
\label{prop:crisp}
Let $T$ be a decision table in which every granule $g$ induces a constant
decision (a crisp decision subtable after granulation), and let
$\pi_{\mathrm{raw}}(T)$ be its unsmoothed empirical granular table. Then the
Pawlak positive region is $POS(T)=\{t:\ \text{the granule of } t \text{ is
deterministic}\}$, the classical degree is
$\gamma_P(T)=|POS(T)|/|U|$, and $\gamma(\pi_{\mathrm{raw}}(T))=\gamma_P(T)$.
In general, for $\beta\in(0,1]$ and any granular table $\pi$, the
variable-precision degree
$\gamma_\beta=\sum_{g:\,c_g\ge\beta}\pi_g$ counts rows whose confidence
reaches $\beta$, and
$\gamma(\pi)=\sum_g \pi_g c_g \ge \beta\,\gamma_\beta$, with equality iff
every occupied row has confidence exactly $\beta$ (rows with $c_g<\beta$
contribute $\pi_g c_g>0$, since $c_g\ge 1/K$ on occupied rows). Thus
$\gamma(\pi)$ is a
confidence-weighted extension of the $\beta$-positive region measure: it
replaces the binary threshold at $\beta$ by the full confidence profile.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Inversion invariance of $\gamma$]\label{prop:inv}
Let the pair $(x_i, x_d)$ satisfy a deterministic relation with granule map
$\varphi$ (i.e.\ $x_d$'s granule is a function $\varphi$ of $x_i$'s granule),
and let the output channel be re-encoded by an order-reversing permutation
of its granules, $r(v)=K-1-v$ (with symmetric quantiles, granulation
commutes with this recoding). Then $\gamma$ is unchanged, $\rho$ drops by
the mass of the rows whose modal rule changed (to its floor when every
occupied row changes), $|\delta|$ is unchanged, and the sign of $\delta$
flips.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Monotonicity of $\gamma$ under decision-granule coarsening]
\label{prop:granular}
Let $\pi$ be a granular decision table over $K$ decision granules, with row
occupancies $\pi_g$ ($\sum_g \pi_g = 1$) and conditional rows
$\pi_{\cdot\mid g}$. Let $T'$ be the coarsened table obtained by replacing
the decision granules by a partition of them into $K' < K$ groups, leaving
the condition granules untouched, so that
$\pi'_{v'\mid g} = \sum_{v \in C_{v'}} \pi_{v\mid g}$ and $\pi'_g = \pi_g$.
Then $\gamma(\pi') \geq \gamma(\pi)$, with equality if and only if in every
occupied row no coarsened group carries more mass than the row's modal
column, that is
$\max_{v'} \sum_{v \in C_{v'}} \pi_{v\mid g} = \max_v \pi_{v\mid g}$.
Equivalently: the group containing the modal column holds no mass outside
that column, and every other group sums to at most the modal column's mass.
\end{proposition}

With these properties in place, the connection to Pawlak's framework
is precise in three senses.

\textbf{(C1) Crisp-table correspondence.} On a decision table whose
granules induce constant decisions, the modal confidence of every occupied
granule is one, and $\gamma$ equals the classical degree
$|POS(T)|/|U|$ measured on the same positive region
(Proposition~\ref{prop:crisp}). This establishes that $\gamma$ is
a strict generalisation of Pawlak's degree: it recovers the classical
measure when the table is crisp and degrades gracefully as noise blurs
granule boundaries. The correspondence in
Proposition~\ref{prop:crisp} holds for the raw table
$\pi_{\mathrm{raw}}(T)$; the operational degree is computed on the
$\alpha$-smoothed, IPF-standardised table $\pi_{\mathrm{sm}}$, on which a
crisp row with $n_g$ observations has smoothed confidence
$(n_g+\alpha/K)/(n_g+\alpha)$ before re-standardisation, so
$\gamma(\pi_{\mathrm{sm}})$ approaches the Pawlak value
as row counts grow rather than attaining it exactly.

\textbf{(C2) Variable-precision reading.} Ziarko's $\beta$-positive
region $\mathrm{POS}_\beta$ counts granules whose conditional confidence
$c_g$ reaches $\beta$. The soft degree replaces this binary threshold by
the full confidence profile: $\gamma = \sum_g \pi_g c_g$ is the
occupancy-weighted integral of the confidence spectrum. When $\beta=1$,
$\gamma_\beta$ equals the crisp-table degree; when $\beta<1$,
$\gamma_\beta \leq \gamma / \beta$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:crisp}).
The advantage of the soft form on real-valued series is that row
confidences concentrate near $1/K$ under independence, so the floor is
exactly $1/K$ and any excursion above it is interpretable as dependence,
whereas a fixed $\beta$ either empties or floods the positive region
depending on the noise level.

\textbf{(C3) Boundary-region decomposition.} The Pawlak boundary
region $BN(T) = U \setminus POS(T)$ consists of objects whose granule
membership is ambiguous. In the soft reading, $\gamma$ quantifies the
degree to which the boundary has been resolved: $\gamma = 1/K$ means the
boundary is fully unresolved (maximum uncertainty), and $\gamma = 1$ means
the boundary is fully resolved (each granule maps to a unique decision).
The three companion statistics ($\rho, \delta, D_{JS}$) characterise
\emph{how} the boundary moves when the relation changes: $\rho$ tracks
whether the modal rule within each boundary cell has flipped, $\delta$
tracks whether the direction of statistical association (not a causal
direction) has reversed, and $D_{JS}$
measures the redistribution of conditional mass within the boundary.
This decomposition is the theoretical basis for typed alarms
(\emph{breakdown} = modal mass shrinks ($\gamma$ drops); \emph{inversion} =
modal mass conserved while the internal structure reverses; \emph{emergence} =
modal confidence rises above the independence floor).

\textbf{Where rough-set theory specifically contributes.} The granular
decision-table construction is not cosmetic labelling; three steps
depend on it. First, the equal-frequency granulation $\Phi^K$ defines
an indiscernibility relation on real-valued channels; the contingency
table is the cardinality of the corresponding granule-level partition,
which is what makes the statistics computable by integer counting and
the block permutation a valid resampling of the partition (rather than
an approximation). Second, $\gamma$ is the soft variable-precision
extension of the Pawlak dependency degree: on crisp tables it equals
$|POS(T)|/|U|$ exactly (Proposition~\ref{prop:crisp}), and its
boundary-region decomposition (C3 above) is Pawlak-specific, with no
counterpart in generic dependency statistics. Third, the dual-channel
graph uses the reference dependency degree as the selection criterion
for the rupture channel; replacing it with a generic association measure
(e.g., absolute Spearman correlation) would select different edges and
change what is monitored. A matched ablation swaps the edge-selection criterion while holding
the detection family, hosts, and FA budget fixed: MI-ranked versus
$\gamma$-ranked candidate edges yield $0.15$ against $0.21$ on
METR-LA, $0.30$ against $0.25$ on PEMS-BAY and $0.36$ against $0.40$ on
Beijing-Air, where the 5{,}520-point test span realises 9--11 events
per seed (99 across ten seeds; the isolation band excludes some planned
injections), so its per-type composition varies. Both selectors rank on
the same tail window, but MI re-estimates its bin quantiles there whereas
the $\gamma$ selector uses full-reference frozen bins, so this compares
the two practical schemes rather than a strict single-factor intervention.
The selection criterion is therefore not load-bearing at
this configuration: both rankings deliver comparable recall, and the
robustness of the monitor to how candidate edges are ranked is itself
a deployment asset. What $\gamma$-selection buys is not recall but
interpretability: the monitored statistic and the selection statistic
are the same quantity.

\textbf{Why not simply discretise and compute classification accuracy?}
With the modal rule re-derived per table, resubstitution accuracy is
numerically this $\gamma$; the contribution of the rough-set reading lies
elsewhere. Three properties distinguish the calibrated monitoring use from
the sample-level accuracy of a fixed discretised classifier.
First, \emph{coarsening monotonicity} (Proposition~\ref{prop:granular}):
$\gamma$ never decreases when decision granules are coarsened, and is
exactly invariant precisely when, in every occupied row, no coarsened
group carries more mass than the row's modal column (the group holding
the modal column must not pool mass from other columns, and no other
group may exceed it), whereas for a fixed rule the pooled-label accuracy can only increase
or stay (an artefact of label pooling), and with the rule re-derived per
table it equals this $\gamma$ and inherits its monotonicity. This makes $\gamma$ a property of the
\emph{dependency structure} rather than of a particular granularity choice.
Second, \emph{exact independence floor}: $\gamma = 1/K$ if and only if every
conditional distribution is uniform on the fully standardised table
(Proposition~\ref{prop:bounds}), giving a calibrated null with no
estimation required beyond the standardisation itself. A fixed-rule accuracy
has no such floor.
Third, \emph{boundary structure}: the complement obeys the strict identity
$1-\gamma=\sum_g \pi_g(1-c_g)$, the occupancy-weighted
non-modal (soft-boundary) mass, which on unsmoothed crisp tables (every occupied row fully determined, so both masses vanish) coincides with the Pawlak boundary-region mass and in general exposes where
decision uncertainty is concentrated; the companion statistics resolve which
kind of boundary change occurred. A fixed-rule accuracy offers no such
region-level accounting.
For a rule re-derived per table these properties coincide with $\gamma$'s, as
noted; the distinction is from the fixed-rule accuracy baseline, and these
three properties are what make the degree a calibrated monitor: inversion
leaves the degree and a refitted accuracy unchanged and is carried by the
direction statistic (\S\ref{sec:ablation}).

\subsection{Dual-channel candidate graph}\label{sec:graph}

Scanning all $n(n-1)|\tau|$ directed pairs at every boundary is self-defeating:
the family of null hypotheses sets the alarm floor far above the signal. \RDSG{}
defines its monitored graph once, at the primary granularity ($K{=}4$, long
window):
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \emph{rupture channel $R$}: the top-$m_s$ unordered pairs by reference
dependency degree, monitored at each pair's best reference lag $\pm1$;
these host breakdowns and inversions;
\item \emph{emergence pool $E$}: the bottom-$m_w$ pairs at lag 0, plus, for
each weak target, subset edges over its $m{=}6$ closest weak partners. These
host emergences, including subset-joint ones.
\end{itemize}
Both channels are evaluated at every (granularity $K$, window $w$) configuration,
but the \emph{graph is shared}: the multi-scale machinery varies the estimator,
not the hypothesis set, so the family stays small and the comparison across
scales is meaningful. Hosts for controlled injections are drawn from the same
graph; a detector is only asked about edges it monitors.

\subsection{Self-scan budget calibration (SBC): thresholds as an input}\label{sec:calib}

For every edge and component we track the boundary-level statistic
$z_e(b)=|s_e(b)-\mu_e|/\sigma_e$, with $\mu_e,\sigma_e$ estimated from
sliding adjacent window pairs inside the reference segment (the same
construction as detection, so no transfer gap is introduced), a floor
$\sigma_e\ge 0.2\,\mathrm{med}_c\sigma$ against degenerate units, and a
unit-validity mask that removes edges whose null behaviour violates the
standardisation (reference self-scan pruning). Three design decisions matter:
\begin{enumerate}
\itemsep2pt
\item \emph{Component families, not max-over-components.} Taking the max over the four components at a single alarm threshold taxes every component with the others' tails; in particular the discrete rule-map component dominates the family maximum. \RDSG{} splits the family per component, assigns each a $\mathrm{FA}/\#\mathrm{components}$ budget (union bound), and alarms when any component exceeds its own threshold.
\item \emph{Rolling-mean evidence.} A true rupture lifts a unit on consecutive boundaries (the window straddles the change), whereas a null peak does not persist. Aggregating $k=4$ consecutive standardised boundary values into the alarm statistic converts persistence into power (a library design option; the evaluated family scan uses per-boundary statistics, S.8).
\item \emph{Self-scan alarm level.} The complete alarm procedure (union over scales and granularities) is executed once on the reference segment, and the alarm threshold is the smallest value whose self-scan alarm-sample rate meets the requested false-alarm budget (samples counted at the method's native scoring resolution; every method in every table is calibrated by this same rule). The requested rate is thus an \emph{input on the reference segment}; its
transfer to the test segment is reported as measured (\S\ref{sec:exp}).
\end{enumerate}
After an alarm, edges are ranked by two-sided normal-tail $p$-values over
the block-permuted statistic change, with Benjamini--Hochberg control over
the family \citep{benjamini1995}; when the procedure yields no rejections,
the ten largest-$z$ edges are reported as an unconstrained ranking rather
than FDR-controlled findings.

\subsection{Event typing}\label{sec:typing}

Alarms are merged into events (network-specific gap tolerance: 512 points
on the 5-minute networks, 256 on the hourly network; S.8). For each event, the
peak edge is identified on the union statistic and typed at an event-aligned
window pair by a channel-aware rule: an $R$-channel event with reference
dependency above the graph median and a significant negative
$\Delta\gamma$ is a \emph{breakdown}; with $|\Delta\gamma|$ below threshold
but rule-map loss, direction flip or conditional divergence above reference
quantiles it is an \emph{inversion}; an $E$-channel event with significant
positive $\Delta\gamma$ is an \emph{emergence} (subset edges attribute
subset-joint emergence). The rule uses only reference-estimated quantities.

\subsection{Margin-preserving injections}\label{sec:inject}

Synthetic evaluation of relational detectors needs anomalies that do not
accidentally create marginal outliers. Our injections re-order \emph{already
observed} values inside a window: inversion re-orders channel $j$'s window
values to a target Spearman correlation with $i$ (the reference correlation's
sign flip, so that inverting an already negative pair is a real change);
breakdown permutes them; emergence aligns them; subset-joint emergence aligns
them to the rank-sum of two partners. The multiset of $j$'s values is
preserved exactly, so any detector whose score is a function of single-channel
marginals has exactly its false-alarm rate as detection rate, which is the
property the mechanism experiment tests.

\subsection{Computational complexity}\label{sec:complexity}

\textbf{Time.} Per detection window, the online cost is
$O(E \cdot C \cdot K^{|A_{\max}|}\times K)$ for the table update and statistic
computation, where $E$ is the number of monitored edges, $C$ the number
of detection components ($C{=}2$), and $K^{|A_{\max}|}\times K$ the largest
contingency table size. For the default configuration ($E{=}116$,
$K{=}4$, $|A|{\leq}2$), this yields approximately $14{,}848$ table
cells per boundary scan. The one-time fit on the reference segment
costs $O(E \cdot B) \cdot K^{|A_{\max}|}\times K$ for the block-permutation
calibration, where $B{=}499$; in practice this takes 2--30
seconds per configuration on a workstation (the S.15 fit column covers
ranking and standardisation only).

\textbf{Space.} The detector stores $E$ contingency tables of size
$K^{|A|} \times K$ (rolling buffer of $w/b$ blocks), $E \times C$  \\
calibration constants, and the frozen granulation bounds ($n \times
(K{-}1)$). At the default settings the online detector runs under 35\,MB
resident on a Raspberry Pi 4B (measured in \S\ref{sec:sens}); the
workstation process reading after fit is 170--171\,MB at $n{=}40$ (S.15).

\textbf{Comparison with baselines.} Partial correlation requires
$O(n^2)$ per window with no calibration state; LOF requires
$O(n \log n)$ per query point after $O(n^2)$ indexing; GDN requires
a one-time training cost of $O(\text{params} \cdot \text{epochs})$
dominating all baselines. \RDSG{} trades a higher per-window cost
for the calibration guarantee: on the gas pipeline (9 channels),
the full pipeline runs in \eSevenOurs{}\,s versus \eSevenCorr{}\,s
for the correlation baseline. On a
Raspberry Pi 4B, the per-window update is 459\,ms at 120 edges,
well within the arrival interval of new detection windows
(\S\ref{sec:sens}).

\footnote{SWaT \citep{goh2017swat,mathur2016swat} was explored but excluded: the publicly

their labelled anomalies are overwhelmingly point-visible (channel-value or

\textbf{Baseline configuration and fairness.} Learned baselines use the
hyperparameters of their original publications (GDN: embedding 16, hidden 64,
window 32; six training epochs with a held-out calibration split, a
resource-constrained budget that may under-train GDN);
statistical baselines use window sizes matched to ours and reference-segment
self-scan thresholds at the same false-alarm budget. Two recent deep scorers
(the Anomaly Transformer \citep{xu2022anomaly} and TranAD \citep{tuli2022tranad})
are included as \emph{compact reimplementations}, not reproductions of the
published pipelines: both are trained on the reference segment only (no
labelled anomalies; window 128, $d_{\rm model}{=}64$, 2 layers, 5 epochs,
ten injection seeds), the AT arm retrains the official architecture with a
simplified jointly-minimised association discrepancy, and the TranAD arm is
a reduced single-encoder reconstructor. Their point-window scores are merged with a 512-point gap (as ours
on the 5-minute networks; our hourly-network gap is 256) and are calibrated by the same rate-based
self-scan, so these rows quantify compact deep scorers under the common
budget, not peak published performance (arms trained up to ten times
longer leave the ordering unchanged; S.14). \RDSG{} hyperparameters
were frozen \emph{once}, before any real-data evaluation, and reused
unchanged everywhere: the structural values ($K$, $w$, and the edge
budgets $m_s$, $m_w$) on the synthetic gate; the standardisation
constants ($\alpha$, IPF iterations) as untuned design defaults
(audited in S.13); the calibration constants ($B$, $q$) by convention
(S.8); no per-dataset tuning was performed.

\textbf{Metrics.} Event-level recall (detection window overlaps the event),
false alarms per day, median detection delay, type accuracy on detected
events. (Point-adjusted F1 is reported in the reproduction package
rather than the main tables.) All per-type
recalls use pooled one-to-one matching for overall recall (each predicted
event credited to at most one true event); per-type recalls condition the
true set on the event type and match against all predicted events (S.8), so
per-type numbers may exceed the overall figure when a single predicted
event spans multiple true types. All methods (ours,
$\gamma$-only ablation, IF/LOF aggregations, GDN \citep{deng2021gdn},
correlation-matrix and partial-correlation divergences, VAR-residual
divergence, granular-MI divergence, bivariate Granger) receive the same
reference-segment self-scan and false-alarm budget. Budgets are matched
as \emph{requested} on the reference segment; realised test-segment
false-alarm rates are reported per method (Table~\ref{tab:e2}) and may
differ across methods.

\textbf{Margin-preserving injections.} Every injection on real dynamics
re-orders the observed values of one channel inside its window, so
marginals are preserved point-wise and per-channel detectors are blind by
construction:

\begin{lemma}[Margin-preserving injections]\label{lemma:margin}
Fix a window $W$ and a target channel $j$. Any injection that outputs a
permutation of the observed values $\{x_j(t)\}_{t\in W}$ leaves the empirical
marginal distribution of $j$ (and of every other channel) unchanged
point-wise. Consequently, for any detector whose per-time score is a fixed function of the
single-channel value (any model or normalisation being fitted strictly
outside the window; scores that pair the target with another channel, such
as prediction residuals, are not covered), the multiset of scores over $W$ is unchanged by
the injection, hence so is the number of threshold exceedances inside $W$ and
the indicator that at least one exceedance occurs in $W$. Detection rates that depend only on these window-level exceedance
counts (for example detecting a window event by requiring at least one
exceedance inside it) are therefore unchanged. Gap-merged event-level
metrics are not claimed invariant (merging can regroup exceedances across
window boundaries); the event recalls in \S\ref{sec:exp} are reported as
measured.
\end{lemma}

\noindent The argument is that a permutation of a multiset preserves its
order statistics, so the score multiset over $W$ is unchanged and every
threshold exceedance stays inside $W$; the time stamps of exceedances may move
within $W$, and gap-merged event counts may split or merge, so event-count
functionals (such as false events per day) are covered empirically rather
than by the lemma. The full proof, with these scope conditions, is given
in the Supplemental Material (S.5).

\textbf{Reproducibility.} Every number in every table is generated from the
result files by a single script; injections, seeds and configurations are
shipped with the code, together with per-seed raw metrics, so that each entry of each table can be traced to its generating run.

\textbf{Evidence positioning.} The recall headlines of the injection suites
come from margin-preserving re-orderings, under which point detectors are
blind by construction (Lemma~\ref{lemma:margin}). On the gas-pipeline attack
log, 50 of 53 attacks carry a point footprint and just
three are purely relational, so the real-world evidence is complementarity
with point monitors plus the inversion case study of \S\ref{sec:e1},
not substitution of them.

\subsection{Injection suites on real networks}\label{sec:e2}

Table~\ref{tab:e2} reports overall event recall on the three real networks.
Three findings. (1) \emph{No method dominates everywhere, and the splits are
informative}: on METR-LA, five-minute traffic whose pairwise associations are dominated
by a shared daily cycle and are close to linear-Gaussian, the windowed
relational monitors fall to $0.02$--$0.12$ under the rate budget (their
overlapping-cadence null tails push thresholds above the signal), the strongest external baseline under this protocol is the compact
Anomaly-Transformer arm at
$0.19$, and \RDSG{} exceeds it at $0.46$ ($p=\eTwoPStrongMetr$).
On Beijing air quality, where station couplings are non-linear and
non-stationary, the partial-correlation divergence is nominally ahead of
the family at $0.24$ against $0.23$ (not significant, $p=\eTwoPStrongBj$), and the $\gamma$-only ablation leads both at
$0.29$; the attribution below explains why. On PEMS-BAY the LOF point aggregation is the strongest
external baseline at the common requested false-alarm budget and \RDSG{}
exceeds it ($0.57$ versus $0.49$); the realised rates
differ ($0.15$ versus $0.42$ false alarms per day, Table~\ref{tab:e2}), so
this is an operating-point comparison at the protocol budget, not a
realised-FA-matched one and not a statement about all thresholds. Several attacks there also create large point deviations, and we
return to this in the gas-pipeline analysis.

\emph{Why the budget counts alarm samples, not merged events.} Counted in
merged events per day (the diagnostic reported in the FA/d columns), the
budget can be arbitraged: densifying the alarms merges them into fewer,
longer events, so a near-continuously alarming configuration can satisfy an
event-counted budget. Measured directly, the point aggregations on METR-LA
reach $0.52$--$0.55$ recall within $0.25$ \emph{event} false alarms per day
through boundary-window blobbing, and the family itself, asked for one
alarm per day on METR-LA, realises $0.03$ event false alarms per day at
$0.54$ recall (seed means). An event-counted budget is therefore arbitrageable under this merging
rule; the alarm-sample rate used here is not, which is why the budget is
defined on it. The effect is visible in the data: calibrating the relational
baselines from sparse independent-pair nulls (44 null samples with a
budget of 10 allowed crossings) inflates their METR-LA recall by factors
from 2.8 to 4.3 (17 for the VAR residual monitor). The inflation is
consistent with boundary-window blobbing under the event metric, though
the per-event mechanism is not separately traced (diagnostics in the
reproduction package); the native-cadence nulls used throughout remove
the inflation. The full
per-method sweeps (requested budgets and test-score quantiles, every seed)
ship with the reproduction package. (2) \emph{The tuple is load-bearing}: on the synthetic gate the
$\gamma$-only ablation collapses on inversion exactly as
Proposition~\ref{prop:blind} predicts ($0.21$ vs.\ $0.47$ pooled recall,
Table~\ref{tab:e0}); on the real networks its overall recall falls to
$0.35$ on METR-LA and $0.49$ on PEMS-BAY (against $0.46$ and $0.57$ for
the full detection family).
Over seeds, the full detection family is significantly better than the $\gamma$-only
ablation on METR-LA (Wilcoxon signed-rank, $n=\eTwoNSeeds$,
$p=\eTwoPgOnlyMetr$) and PEMS-BAY ($p=\eTwoPgOnlyBay$), and the $\gamma$-only ablation
in fact outperforms the full family on Beijing-Air ($p=\eTwoPgOnlyBj$ for
the family-over-$\gamma$ one-sided test; reverse $p\approx 0.009$;
$0.29$ versus $0.23$), the boundary movement of $\gamma$ carrying the
evidence on that dataset; the attribution below quantifies the cost). A component-level
attribution of the alarms explains the Beijing split: there the full
family predicts fewer events than its $\gamma$-only ablation on 8 of
10 seeds (7 versus 9.5 at the median), the direction component's
extra alarms merging events that $\gamma$-only credits separately, a
pattern consistent with the merging mechanism, though the two variants
are calibrated independently so the alarm sets are not nested, so
the family's pooled recall falls below the ablation's. The component that adds true detections on the traffic networks costs
pooled-matching credits here. On METR-LA, 84 of 117 detected events involve
the direction component. Against the strongest \emph{external} baseline
the differences favour \RDSG{} significantly on METR-LA and
PEMS-BAY (one-sided Wilcoxon $p=\eTwoPStrongMetr$ and $p=\eTwoPStrongBay$; both survive Holm across networks and baseline-selection adjustment (Westfall--Young, S.7))
but not on Beijing-Air ($p=\eTwoPStrongBj$), where the strongest
baseline is nominally ahead; per-seed tables,
rank-biserial effect sizes, bootstrap intervals and paired differences
against the strongest external baselines are given in the Supplemental
Material (S.7), with a threats-to-validity summary in S.9. (3) \emph{Point-footprint and windowed-divergence methods are at
their floor on two of three networks}: the deep point-window scorers (Anomaly Transformer, TranAD; Table~\ref{tab:e2}) reach only 0.08--0.22 recall (above the IF/LOF floor but far below the calibrated structural monitor), suggesting that, in this compact form, their association-discrepancy
and reconstruction scores pick up boundary-window point artefacts rather
than dependency changes themselves. IF detects almost
nothing anywhere, and LOF stays at its floor on METR-LA and Beijing-Air
(its PEMS-BAY strength is a point-deviation effect, below), the empirical content of
Lemma~\ref{lemma:margin} for per-channel scores; GDN's floor is likewise an
empirical outcome, though its learned multi-channel score falls outside the
lemma's scope. A sample-level
rough-set outlier baseline (neighbourhood-coverage scores \citep{hu2008neighborhood},
max-aggregated, same budget) sits at
the same floor (Table~\ref{tab:e2}, Rough-NN), as do classical change-point
methods: CUSUM on the dependency-degree statistic reaches $0.05$ recall
(METR-LA, ten seeds) and distance-correlation scoring $0.00$ (PEMS-BAY).
The sample-scoring vs.\ dependence-monitoring distinction is empirical,
not rhetorical.

\textbf{On-device update cost.} To answer whether the rolling dependency-graph
update is deployable at the edge, the online path of \S\ref{sec:calib} was
profiled on a Raspberry Pi 4B (4\,~GB, Cortex-A72, NumPy only, single thread),
using frozen deployment artefacts (quantile bounds, the fixed edge set, and
the calibration constants of \S\ref{sec:calib}) and three update paths: (A)
re-digitise the raw window and rebuild all tables, (B) cache the frozen
digitised blocks and rebuild all tables, and (C) maintain rolling block
tables incrementally. Over 70 measured fragments (5 rounds $\times$ 500
windows per configuration and path), the per-window cost of the deployed
path (C) is
median $459\,~\mathrm{ms}$ ($\mathrm{p95}~461\,~\mathrm{ms}$) on the 120-edge
real40 configuration (fitted edges 104--116; workstation scan
$3.0$--$3.3$\,ms, S.15), about $2.2\,~\mathrm{windows/s}$
of serial steady-state throughput at a peak RSS of $34.4\,~\mathrm{MB}$. Against the arrival interval
of new detection windows (one stride of $b=32$ points at 5-minute
sampling, i.e.\ $\approx 2.7\,~\mathrm{h}$), the margin is four orders of
magnitude: the update
mechanism runs comfortably on edge hardware. Two structural readings
matter. First, the cost is dominated by the per-edge table construction
loop: across the edge sweep ($E=32,60,112,116,120,343$) path (C) grows
from $124$ to $1305\,~\mathrm{ms}$, close to linear in $E$. Second, the
incremental path (C) is $\approx 2.2\times$ \emph{slower} than full rebuild
(A) in this pure-NumPy implementation: its per-block loop overhead exceeds
the saved re-digitisation, while caching the digitised blocks (B) saves
nothing over (A). The rolling table is therefore a memory optimisation
($30\,\%$ less resident state in the smallest configuration), not a speed
optimisation, and a C-level implementation of the block update is the
natural next step if tighter margins are ever needed. One-off deployment
costs (granulation fit, edge-set construction, calibration) were measured
on a workstation ($2.3$--$30\,~\mathrm{s}$ across configurations, see the
repository) and are amortised (scaling to $n{=}2000$: construction
minutes-scale, boundary scan millisecond-scale, S.15); recalibration
triggers discussed in
\S\ref{sec:discussion}.



\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Where Gaussian tools are enough.} On the hourly air-quality network, whose host associations are
mostly weak regardless of functional form, the
partial-correlation divergence is nominally the strongest method there
($0.24$ against the family's $0.23$, not significant), and the $\gamma$-only ablation leads both at $0.29$, a boundary practitioners
should know. On the near-linear 5-minute networks the same Gaussian
monitors fall to their floors under the rate budget, and the granular
machinery earns its complexity there, on non-Gaussian couplings and
marginal robustness. The full family is
ahead of its $\gamma$-only ablation nowhere on air quality, a scope limitation worth stating. The division of
labour is a result, not an embarrassment. A selection guide is tabulated
in S.16, the theory's guarantee boundary in S.9.

\textbf{Short references.} The gas pipeline's 27-minute reference cannot
represent a day of operating modes. Thresholds migrate. Our answer is the
operating curve. A segment-level rolling recalibration prototype that re-runs
the calibration whenever a test segment completes without an alarm does
not help. On this log every segment contains attacks, the recalibration
never engages, and its curve is no better than the static one at low false
alarm budgets ($\eWFiveRoll{}$ vs.\ $\eWFiveStatic{}$ at $\le 5$ alarms/day).
Recalibration presupposes quiet stretches that attack-dense logs do not
offer; hence we report curves rather than recalibrated points.

\textbf{Typing.} The event-aligned typing rule remains the weakest component:
across the ten-seed injection suites it stays near chance ($0.28$ on
METR-LA, $0.26$ on PEMS-BAY and $0.30$ on Beijing air quality; chance
$0.25$--$0.33$). The component-triggered variant of \S\ref{sec:e2} lifts METR-LA
to $0.49$ (inversion $0.75$), with Beijing-Air at $0.36$.
Even so, a component that reaches usable accuracy on
one dataset and not on the others is not yet a typing module. Readers
should treat the type label as indicative. Component-level attribution
(which statistic fired, on which edge) is reliable by construction; the
diagnostic type label stays auxiliary. The
residual is structured, and the failure chain is documented in the
repository.

\textbf{What the gate taught us.} Three of the statistical choices in \S\ref{sec:method} (soft $\gamma$, copula standardisation, per-component families) exist because a naive
assembly of the same ideas measurably failed. A constant artefactual
dependency of $0.39$ arose from margin occupancy. The discrete rule-map
component pushed the family maximum to $14$. Most instructively, an earlier
candidate graph ranked two-thirds noise into its top-``strong'' pairs through
short-window ranking, so inversion injections landed on independent pairs and
therefore \emph{looked} detectable by $\gamma$ alone. Cleaning the graph
definition exposed the true blind spot: with a correct candidate graph, $\gamma$-only inversion recall falls from an
artefactual $0.54$ to $\eZeroGammaInv$, while the full tuple retains
$\eZeroRdsInv$. The failure chain is documented in the repository.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We turned the rough-set dependency degree into a monitored object: a
dependency graph whose edges carry a four-tuple of copula-standardised
granular statistics, maintained through a dual-channel candidate graph. Its
calibration machinery makes the alarms falsifiable. Block-permuted nulls, a
self-scanned false-alarm budget (SBC), component families, and edge-attributed
events all ship with the monitor. The detector is
blind by design to what marginals say and sensitive only to the
dependence structure they leave unsaid. The
experiments show the consequences of that asymmetry on synthetic gates,
the three real networks, and two labelled ICS logs. Future work targets three directions. First, rolling recalibration
for long deployments whose quiet stretches let the reference segment
track operating modes; the attack-dense regime of \S\ref{sec:discussion}
is precisely where this fails today. Second, extending subset edges to
higher-order interactions beyond pairs. Third, integrating the typed
alarm labels with operator decision support, so a \emph{breakdown} can
trigger a different inspection than an \emph{emergence} once typing
reliability is established.
